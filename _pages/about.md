I am a PhD Candidate in Biology at the University of Virginia. I received my B.Sc. in Wildlife Biology from Cal Poly Humboldt, where I was primiarily interested in conservation biology along human-wildlife contact zones, with a focus on mammals and birds. I also participated in the Summer Systematics Institute, an REU program hosted by the California Academy of Sciences, where I performed morphometric and genomic analyses on saw-scaled vipers (*Echis* spp.) to reveal cryptic diversity within the genus.

My work in the [Schield Lab](https://schieldlab.org/) aims to describe the genomic consequences of coevolution in a diffusely evolving predator-prey system. Using rattlesnakes (*Crotalus* spp.) and a variety of varably resistant rodent prey species, I seek to understand how various forms of natural selection have influenced the evolution of complex interacting traits in a system where the imposed selective pressures varies greatly between antagonists. 

I use comparative genomics, population genetics, and molecular assays to describe the recipricol coevolution of venom and resistance traits across a continuum of divergence to describe the genetic basis of these traits, how they are shaped by natural selection, and the functionaly outcome of coevolutionary antagonism.
